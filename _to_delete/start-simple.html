Why don't the numbers agree?

You'll hear Petaluma's street score as anywhere from 42 to 52, and the repair backlog as anywhere from $100M to $200M. Both ranges are "true" — they're just measuring different moments or different things. [SOURCE: cite each figure + what it measures]

What is "PCI," anyway?

Pavement Condition Index — a 0–100 score from a standardized street-by-street survey. [SOURCE: ASTM method citation] Roughly: the higher the number, the less it costs to fix.

Why not just fix the worst streets first?

Streets already below about PCI 25 are, on purpose, not the priority. [SOURCE: ] The streets that cost the most to neglect are actually the ones in the 40–60 band. [SOURCE: ]

So how does the city pick which streets to fix?

Two layers: a cost-effectiveness ranking from the city's pavement-management software (StreetSaver), and a set of published discretionary factors layered on top. [SOURCE: ]

  • Collision history [SOURCE: ]
  • Proximity to schools/parks [SOURCE: ]
  • Utility work coordination [SOURCE: ]
  • Community input [SOURCE: ]

The equity wrinkle

The ranking system weights streets partly by how much traffic they carry — which means quiet residential streets rank behind busy arterials, by design. [SOURCE: ]

Is the city keeping pace with what Measure U promised?

One public commenter's tally puts completed work at roughly 25–30 of 396 lane miles (6–7%). This is a public commenter's figure, not an official city tally. [SOURCE: ]

Separately, and not to be confused with the above: the Oversight Committee has recommended dedicating 25% of Measure U to roads — a funding-priority dispute, not an execution-delay story. [SOURCE: ]

What does it cost just to measure all this?

The software itself is cheap. [SOURCE: StreetSaver license cost] The real expense is the biennial condition survey — and MTC's P-TAP program covers most of that. [SOURCE: ]

The takeaway

Same story as everywhere else on this site: a one-year budget clock deciding what to do about a fifty-year infrastructure clock. See Two Clocks.

What we're asking the city for: the actual StreetSaver scenario outputs / 5-Year Paving Plan model runs, as a public record — plus a plain clarification of which PCI and backlog figures are current and why they differ. [SOURCE: public records request reference, once filed]
